## 1. Drama 3 is here; hosted editing is still limited

Fish Audio [launched Drama 3 on October 13](https://fish.audio/blog/fish-audio-drama-3/). Directed TTS is available on web, mobile and API. Hosted interactive revision and audio inpainting remain invite-only, with an editing API forthcoming. These are different access levels.

The [API documentation](https://docs.fish.audio/features/text-to-speech/drama-3) describes word-scoped performance tags and multi-speaker dialogue. One practical trap: a missing or misspelled model header falls back to S2.1 Pro. Language switching is experimental.

The [model card](https://huggingface.co/fishaudio/drama-3) describes a 35B mixture of experts with about 3B active per token and hybrid attention. Its local [installation requirements](https://speech.fish.audio/install/) specify at least 48 GB FP8 GPU memory on NVIDIA. The [October 13 Community License](https://huggingface.co/fishaudio/drama-3/blob/main/LICENSE) imposes revenue and hosted-service conditions; the Hub's research-license badge is stale. Open weights is the accurate label.

**Evidence:** Fish's pronunciation headline is a vendor report about a specific independent benchmark. [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/models) tests difficult English spans judged by humans, not overall acting or every supported language. Numerical placement was not independently reverified in this delta. No model was run.

**Creator implication:** worth evaluating for directed dialogue, without assuming hosted word replacement is generally available. [OpenArt now lists Drama 3](https://openart.ai/ai-model/); its integration timestamp, plans and credits were not verified.

**Visual proposal:** “3B active does not mean a 3B-sized model.” Animate expert routing and separate weight storage from token work and cache growth. Controls: context length, precision, hypothetical expert routing and full-attention fraction. Status: proposed, with explicit assumptions rather than a VRAM benchmark. Added as item eight in the graphics queue.

## 2. ComfyUI 0.40.0 packages new camera and workflow tools

The [October 13 release](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/ComfyUI/releases/tag/v0.40.0) includes camera metadata/prompt nodes, standard 3D outputs, LoRA stacks and new creative-service integrations. The [CameraAngle change](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/ComfyUI/pull/16251), merged October 6 and now in this tagged release, maps horizontal angle, vertical angle and zoom to camera data and shot text. It does not enforce geometric fidelity in an arbitrary generator. Partner nodes do not imply local weights or free generation.

**Visual proposal:** orbit a synthetic subject and compare the exact projected camera with its coarse language description. Scrub azimuth/elevation/zoom and expose where a prompt label changes while geometry moves continuously. Proposed, not implemented.

## 3. NVIDIA VFX effects are available as Comfy nodes

Comfy's [October 13 announcement](https://blog.comfy.org/p/nvidia-vfx-nodes-for-video-upscaling) introduces an official workflow for upscaling, frame interpolation and SDR-to-HDR conversion. The [repository](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/ComfyUI_Nvidia_VFX_Nodes) requires NVIDIA RTX and CUDA PyTorch cu130 or newer; frame generation requires RTX 40 series or later. HDR output still needs the appropriate downstream color encoding. This is a workflow integration, not a new foundation model or verified quality win.

**Visual proposal:** compare sample density, frame timing and display encoding as three separate operations. Increasing frame rate cannot recover observed motion that was never captured. Proposed synthetic demonstration only.
